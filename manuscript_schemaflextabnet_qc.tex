\begin{abstract}
Public cardiovascular-risk benchmarks are routinely used to report accuracies above 90\%, yet duplicated records, cross-cohort record overlap, and schema loss during dataset merging receive little scrutiny. We audit six open data sources (74{,}013 non-overlapping patients after quality control) and show that (i)~723 of 1{,}025 rows (70.5\%) in a widely used Kaggle heart-disease copy are exact duplicates, so a random 80/20 split places 97.1\% of test rows in the training set and inflates accuracy to 98.5\%, versus 77.1\% after deduplication; (ii)~all 270 UCI Statlog records match Cleveland records under a clinical signature, indicating potential duplicate derivation and warranting exclusion against leakage; (iii)~inner-join harmonization discards unshared features and drives zero-shot cross-cohort AUROC down to 0.535--0.713; and (iv)~duplicates are too few (1.02\%) to explain a published 90.1\% accuracy on a 71{,}025-row pool, where an empirical 70/15/15 reproduction using standard tabular models yields approximately 73\% with or without them. We propose \sfn{}, a feature-tokenizing transformer that models only the variables each cohort actually observes, trained with feature dropout, an auxiliary hypertension concept head and a monotonicity penalty, and deployed with temperature-scaled conformal abstention. Under leave-one-cohort-out (LOCO) evaluation across five seeds, it reaches a mean AUROC of $0.7635 \pm 0.0077$ (seed-42: 0.7662), versus $0.6154 \pm 0.0054$ for inner-join LightGBM and $0.7405 \pm 0.0094$ for NaN-aware union-schema LightGBM; against the latter, no per-cohort difference is statistically significant after Holm correction, indicating superiority was not established though formal statistical equivalence is not proven. Under 50\% test-time feature missingness, \sfn{} achieves 0.712 $\pm$ 0.005 AUROC versus 0.684 $\pm$ 0.007 for native-NaN LightGBM and 0.726 $\pm$ 0.002 for median-imputed LightGBM across repeated seeds, demonstrating that tree baselines with imputation remain competitive under missingness. Conformal sets calibrated on source cohorts under-cover on held-out hospitals (averaging 70.7\% on Framingham and 87.7--88.7\% on tertiary cohorts against a nominal 90\%), bounded strictly by accepted accuracy under Eq.~(\ref{eq:conformal_bound}) and dropping to 70.7\% on Framingham due to outcome construct divergence between cross-sectional angiographic CAD and 10-year prospective incident events.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{ardiovascular} disease (CVD) causes roughly 17.9 million deaths per year worldwide. Machine-learning risk models are attractive for screening, but accuracies published on public benchmarks frequently exceed what noisy routine records can support, and they rarely survive transfer to a new hospital. Two well-documented sources of optimism are leakage between training and test data~\cite{kapoor2023} and near-duplicate records in benchmark files~\cite{barz2020}. A third, specific to multi-source clinical work, is the harmonization step itself: merging cohorts through an inner join keeps only the columns present everywhere and silently discards the most diagnostic variables of specialist cohorts (chest-pain type, exercise ECG response, vessel counts).

A recent study, \htn{}~\cite{mahmud2026}, reports 90.1\% accuracy and 93.6\% AUROC on a merger of two Kaggle datasets, using a transformer encoder fused with engineered meta-features (BMI, pulse pressure, and a cholesterol--glucose product). Motivated by the gap between that figure and what we observe on clean data, this paper makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A data audit} of six public sources quantifying exact-duplicate contamination, cross-cohort patient overlap and physiologically implausible records, with a deterministic plausibility gatekeeper and a reproducible audit log.
\item \textbf{A reproducibility analysis} of~\cite{mahmud2026} that documents internal inconsistencies, bounds what the duplicate issue can explain, and reports a 70/15/15 reproduction of the published setup.
\item \textbf{\sfn{}}, a schema-flexible tokenizing transformer that trains across cohorts with non-identical columns, with physiological regularizers and conformal abstention.
\item \textbf{A leave-one-cohort-out benchmark} against strong baselines, including a NaN-aware union-schema gradient-boosting model, with Holm-corrected DeLong tests, ablations, missingness stress tests against native-NaN trees, a conformal cohort-shift audit, and subgroup, federated and decision-curve analyses.
\end{enumerate}
The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work, Section~\ref{sec:data} describes data and quality control, Section~\ref{sec:audit} presents the audit, Section~\ref{sec:method} the method, Section~\ref{sec:protocol} the protocol, Section~\ref{sec:results} the results, Section~\ref{sec:discussion} the discussion and limitations, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Tabular deep learning.} FT-Transformer~\cite{gorishniy2021}, TabNet~\cite{arik2021} and SAINT~\cite{somepalli2021} show that attention over feature tokens is competitive on tabular data, although gradient-boosted trees such as LightGBM~\cite{ke2017} remain strong baselines. Our tokenizer follows the feature-token idea but treats the \emph{set of observed features} as the input, so unobserved columns are masked rather than imputed.

\textbf{Domain knowledge in models.} Concept bottleneck models~\cite{koh2020} supervise intermediate, human-interpretable concepts, and monotonic models~\cite{gupta2016} encode the prior that risk should not fall as a harmful marker rises. We use both as soft regularizers rather than hard architectural constraints.

\textbf{Calibration and abstention.} Temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017} and conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005,angelopoulos2023} provide calibrated probabilities and set-valued predictions with finite-sample coverage under exchangeability. That assumption fails under cohort shift~\cite{tibshirani2019,barber2023}, which matters for our setting and which we measure directly (Section~\ref{sec:conformal}).

\textbf{Reporting standards.} TRIPOD+AI~\cite{collins2024} and leakage checklists~\cite{kapoor2023} motivate the audit-first design of this work.

\section{Data and Quality Control}\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Sources}
We use six public sources: UCI Heart Disease from four institutions (Cleveland, Hungarian, Switzerland, VA Long Beach)~\cite{dua2019,detrano1989}; the Kaggle heart-disease copy (1{,}025 rows); the Sulianova cardiovascular dataset (70{,}000 examination records); a Framingham teaching subset (4{,}240 records)~\cite{dawber1951}; UCI Statlog Heart (270 records); and the Z-Alizadeh Sani angiography cohort (303 records; 56 clinical and laboratory variables)~\cite{alizadehsani2013}.

\subsection{Canonical feature registry}\label{sec:schema}
The multi-cohort canonical schema registry defines $M=14$ clinical features:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{8 continuous variables}: age (\texttt{age\_years}), systolic blood pressure (\texttt{systolic\_bp}), diastolic blood pressure (\texttt{diastolic\_bp}), body mass index (\texttt{bmi}), pulse pressure (\texttt{pulse\_pressure} = $\mathrm{SBP}-\mathrm{DBP}$), total serum cholesterol (\texttt{chol\_mgdl}), maximum heart rate achieved (\texttt{max\_hr\_thalach}), and exercise-induced ST depression (\texttt{st\_depression\_oldpeak}).
\item \textbf{6 categorical/ordinal variables}: biological sex (\texttt{sex\_male}), Adult Treatment Panel III cholesterol risk tier (\texttt{chol\_tier}), fasting glucose / diabetes classification tier (\texttt{gluc\_tier}), chest pain symptom presentation (\texttt{chest\_pain\_type}), exercise-induced angina (\texttt{exercise\_angina}), and smoking history (\texttt{smoker}).
\end{itemize}
In addition, the registry defines \textbf{one derived auxiliary clinical concept label} ($C=1$): Stage~2 hypertension (\texttt{concept\_hypertension}: $\mathrm{SBP}\ge 140$ or $\mathrm{DBP}\ge 90$~mmHg), which provides auxiliary multi-task supervision for interpretive bottleneck regularization during training ($\lambda_{\mathrm{concept}}=0.10$) and is not supplied as an input feature.
The baseline inner-join merge restricts input features to the $M_{\mathrm{shared}}=5$ clinical attributes universally recorded across all cohorts (\texttt{age\_years}, \texttt{sex\_male}, \texttt{systolic\_bp}, \texttt{chol\_tier}, \texttt{smoker}), whereas the union schema provides access to all $M=14$ canonical features. Variables that a cohort does not record are preserved as structurally \emph{absent} tokens masked in self-attention, rather than statically imputed. \subsection{Plausibility gatekeeper}
Instead of a statistical $|Z|>3$ filter, records are rejected if they violate physiological bounds (age 18--110~y; SBP 60--260; DBP 40--160~mmHg; BMI 12--70; cholesterol 80--650~mg/dL; heart rate 50--230~bpm; ST depression 0--10~mm) or hemodynamic consistency (SBP $-$ DBP $\ge$ 10~mmHg). Exact patient-level duplicates are removed. Table~\ref{tab:audit} gives the audit log. The eight clean cohorts sum to 74{,}283 records; excluding the 270 Statlog rows whose clinical measurement signatures match Cleveland records leaves 74{,}013 non-overlapping patients, which is the pool used throughout the paper. Sulianova contributes 92.6\% of these (68{,}568 of 74{,}013), a fact that shapes several conclusions below.

\section{Reproducibility Audit}\label{sec:audit}
\subsection{Duplicate leakage and cohort overlap}
The Kaggle heart copy contains only 302 unique patients; the remaining 723 rows (70.5\%) are exact copies. Under a random 80/20 split, 199 of 205 test rows (97.1\%) have an identical twin in the training set. Evaluating on that split yields near-perfect scores that vanish once duplicates are removed (Table~\ref{tab:leak}). A signature match on age, sex, resting blood pressure, cholesterol, peak heart rate and ST depression further shows that all 270 Statlog records match Cleveland records under this clinical-feature signature. While matching clinical measurements alone cannot conclusively verify unique biological patient identity without institutional identifiers, this exact match strongly indicates record-level overlap or duplicate derivation from the same underlying registry; treating these records as overlapping is a necessary and prudent precaution against data leakage, so we excluded the overlap from every cross-cohort experiment.

\subsection{Observations on the published \htn{} results}
We did not have access to code or splits for~\cite{mahmud2026}, and we do not claim to know why its figures differ from ours. We report only what can be checked from the article itself, plus a reproduction of its stated split.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Inconsistent confusion matrices.} The text describes a matrix with 980/1{,}100 negatives and 990/1{,}100 positives correct (accuracy 89.5\%), whereas Fig.~2 of that article shows 4{,}795/533/395/4{,}930 on 10{,}653 samples, implying accuracy 91.3\% and positive-class recall 0.926, against 0.90 in its Table~1.\footnote{The project implementation reports list an alternative matrix (5{,}044/4{,}688/444/477). We use the values from the published LaTeX, which are consistent with the ``balanced 5{,}328 vs.\ 5{,}325'' description. Readers should verify against the published figure.}
\item \textbf{Class balance.} The test set is described as balanced (5{,}328 vs.\ 5{,}325), while the preprocessing section describes a roughly 1:2 minority-to-majority imbalance with class weights 0.45/0.55.
\item \textbf{Test-set size.} 10{,}653 equals 15\% of 71{,}025, the sum of the raw rows of the two Kaggle files ($1{,}025+70{,}000$). This is consistent with the duplicated 1{,}025-row copy having been used, and with little row removal despite the described outlier filtering.
\item \textbf{Limits of the duplicate explanation.} The 723 duplicate rows represent $723/71{,}025=1.02\%$ of the pooled set. In an illustrative fixed-prediction calculation where predictions on non-duplicate records remain invariant, even if every duplicate row were classified correctly, raising their accuracy from about 73\% to 100\% would lift overall accuracy by only about $0.0102\times(1-0.73)\approx 0.30$ percentage points. While duplicate retention can also perturb model weights, split compositions and predictions on other records, our controlled retraining experiments confirm this order of magnitude: accuracy changes by only $+0.30$ points (73.18\% vs.\ 73.48\%). Duplicates therefore cannot by themselves explain 90.1\% accuracy on a pool that is 98.6\% Sulianova, where well-tuned models in our benchmark reach roughly 73--76\%.
\item \textbf{Engineered features are not architecture-specific.} Adding BMI, pulse pressure and tier features changes AUROC by $+0.0014$ (MLP) to $+0.0124$ (logistic regression) across seven model families (Section~\ref{sec:pooled}). Work~\cite{mahmud2026} states that baselines were retrained under the same pipeline, so we do not allege unequal treatment; we show only that the features help every model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reproduction of the 70/15/15 setup}
We explicitly distinguish between (i)~a reproduction of the reported data-processing and splitting protocol using standard tabular baselines, (ii)~a baseline replication, and (iii)~an architecture-faithful reproduction of the original neural model. Lacking the original code, random seeds, and split indices of~\cite{mahmud2026}, we implemented the stated 70/15/15 train/validation/test protocol on the raw 71{,}025-row pool, the deduplicated pool (70{,}302), and Sulianova alone (70{,}000), using the same hemodynamic filtering and standard LightGBM, Random Forest, and MLP baselines with default hyperparameters and the engineered meta-features described in~\cite{mahmud2026}. Table~\ref{tab:repro} shows that all three configurations land at about 73\% accuracy and 0.795--0.799 AUROC. Keeping the duplicates changes accuracy by $+0.30$ points, matching the fixed-prediction estimate. We could not reproduce 90.1\% with these features and this protocol using standard tabular models. Because original neural weights and splits remain unreleased, we cannot determine whether the original transformer architecture achieved 90.1\% through unstated preprocessing, hyperparameter optimization, or reporting discrepancies; we recommend release of splits and code.

\subsection{Schema-flexible tokenizer}
For patient $i$ with observed feature set $\mathcal{F}_i$, each observed feature $j\in\mathcal{F}_i$ becomes a token
\begin{equation}
z_{ij}=e_{\mathrm{name}}(f_j)+w_j\,x_{ij}+b_j,
\end{equation}
where $e_{\mathrm{name}}(f_j)\in\mathbb{R}^d$ is a learned feature-identity embedding and $(w_j,b_j)$ is a feature-specific linear projection (categorical variables use learned value embeddings). Continuous inputs are z-scored with training statistics. A learnable \texttt{[CLS]} token is prepended and a key-padding mask removes unobserved features from attention, so no inner join, zero-fill or imputation is needed. The encoder has 2 layers, 4 heads, width $d=64$, feed-forward width 128 and dropout 0.10.

\subsection{Feature dropout}
During training each observed token is dropped with probability $p_{\mathrm{mask}}=0.20$, simulating hospitals that supply only part of the panel and forcing the encoder not to rely on any single variable.

\subsection{Concept head and monotonicity}
The \texttt{[CLS]} representation also predicts Stage~2 hypertension, giving $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{concept}}$ (binary cross-entropy). Monotonicity is encouraged in two ways for the set of harmful markers $\mathcal{M}=\{$age, SBP, cholesterol, ST depression$\}$. First, the logit contains a residual branch with softplus-constrained, hence non-negative, weights:
\begin{equation}
\hat z_i=\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{out}}^{\top}\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{CLS}}_i+b_0+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{softplus}(w_k)\,x_{ik}\,\mathbb{I}[x_{ik}\ \text{observed}],
\end{equation}
with $\hat y_i=\sigma(\hat z_i)$. Second, an input-gradient penalty computed by automatic differentiation,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mono}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(-\frac{\partial \hat y_i}{\partial x_{i,k}}\right)\mathbb{I}[x_{i,k}\ \text{observed}].
\end{equation}
The total loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}}+\lambda_1\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{concept}}+\lambda_2\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mono}},
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_1=0.10$ and $\lambda_2=1.20$. Because the attention path is unconstrained, neither mechanism guarantees monotonicity; we therefore \emph{measure} violations (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Calibration and conformal procedure}\label{sec:conformal_theory}
To guarantee valid distribution-free coverage under exchangeability, the model-fitting validation split and the conformal calibration split must be strictly separated. Reusing labeled calibration data to fit post-hoc probability calibration (e.g., temperature scaling) or early stopping introduces optimization bias and invalidates standard split-conformal exchangeability. We therefore enforce a strict \emph{three-way source partitioning protocol}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Training split} ($D_{\mathrm{train}}$, 70\% of source pool): used exclusively for optimizing network parameters $\theta$.
\item \emph{Validation split} ($D_{\mathrm{val}}$, 15\% of source pool): used for early stopping checkpoint selection and fitting the scalar temperature $T^*$ via L-BFGS to minimize negative log-likelihood on uncalibrated logits $z(x)$.
\item \emph{Calibration split} ($D_{\mathrm{cal}}$, 15\% of source pool): a strictly disjoint, untouched partition with $n$ patients. With $(\theta, T^*)$ frozen, calibrated probabilities $p_y(x) = \sigma(z(x)/T^*)$ yield non-conformity scores $s(x,y) = 1 - p_y(x) \in [0, 1]$.
\end{enumerate}
Because $D_{\mathrm{cal}}$ is disjoint from both $D_{\mathrm{train}}$ and $D_{\mathrm{val}}$, the score function $s(x,y)$ is deterministic with respect to $D_{\mathrm{cal}}$. Conditional on the fitted model, scores on $D_{\mathrm{cal}}$ and on any i.i.d.\ test point $(X_{n+1}, Y_{n+1}) \sim P_{\mathrm{source}}$ are strictly exchangeable.
At nominal error rate $\alpha=0.10$ ($1-\alpha=90\%$ target coverage), the finite-sample adjusted conformal quantile threshold is computed on $n$ calibration samples as:
\begin{equation}
\hat{q} = \mathrm{Quantile}\left(\{s(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n; \; \frac{\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha) \rceil}{n}\right).
\end{equation}
The prediction set for a test patient $x$ is:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}(x) = \{y \in \{0, 1\} : s(x, y) \le \hat{q}\} = \{y \in \{0, 1\} : p_y(x) \ge 1 - \hat{q}\}.
\end{equation}
Under source exchangeability, $\Pr[Y \in \mathcal{C}(X)] \ge 1 - \alpha$. A case is \emph{accepted} when $|\mathcal{C}(x)|=1$ (decisive singleton with point prediction $\hat{y} = \arg\max_y p_y(x)$), and \emph{referred/abstained} when $|\mathcal{C}(x)| \in \{0, 2\}$. Mondrian conformal calibration computes $\hat{q}_g$ independently for biological sex $g \in \{\mathrm{Male}, \mathrm{Female}\}$.

\textbf{Mathematical upper bound on conformal coverage.} For binary classification on $N$ evaluation patients, prediction sets satisfy $|\mathcal{C}(x_i)|\in\{0,1,2\}$. Let $N_0 = |\{i : |\mathcal{C}(x_i)|=0\}|$ denote empty sets, $N_1 = |\{i : |\mathcal{C}(x_i)|=1\}|$ singleton sets (accepted predictions; $r_{\mathrm{acc}}=N_1/N$), and $N_2 = |\{i : |\mathcal{C}(x_i)|=2\}|$ uncertain pair sets ($\mathcal{C}(x_i)=\{0,1\}$), with $N=N_0+N_1+N_2$. Accepted patients achieve accuracy $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{acc}} = N_{1,\mathrm{corr}}/N_1$. Crucially, for every incorrectly predicted accepted patient, $\mathcal{C}(x_i)=\{1-y_i\}$, so the true label $y_i$ is strictly absent. Empty sets likewise contain no labels. Therefore, true label coverage $y_i\in\mathcal{C}(x_i)$ is satisfied only by correct singletons and pair sets: $N_{\mathrm{cov}} = N_{1,\mathrm{corr}} + N_2 = (N_1 - N_{1,\mathrm{err}}) + (N - N_0 - N_1) = N - N_0 - N_{1,\mathrm{err}}$. Dividing by $N$ yields the exact coverage identity:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Coverage} = \frac{N_{\mathrm{cov}}}{N} = 1 - \frac{N_0}{N} - r_{\mathrm{acc}}(1 - \mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{acc}}) \le 1 - r_{\mathrm{acc}}(1 - \mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{acc}}),
\label{eq:conformal_bound}
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if no empty prediction sets occur ($N_0=0$). Any empirical coverage value that exceeds $1 - r_{\mathrm{acc}}(1 - \mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{acc}})$ violates this identity and is mathematically inconsistent. In leave-one-cohort-out use, source calibration violates exchangeability ($P_{\mathrm{source}}\neq P_{\mathrm{target}}$); we measure the resulting coverage drop in Section~\ref{sec:conformal}.

\subsection{Schema-aware federated averaging}
In the federated simulation each hospital node $h$ observes a feature subset $\mathcal{F}_h$. Parameters shared across features (transformer blocks, \texttt{[CLS]}, heads) are averaged over all nodes, weighted by local sample size $n_h$:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{shared}}\leftarrow\sum_h \frac{n_h}{\sum_{h'}n_{h'}}\,\theta^{(h)}_{\mathrm{shared}}.
\end{equation}
Feature-specific parameters $\theta_j=(e_{\mathrm{name}}(f_j),w_j,b_j)$ are averaged only over the nodes $H_j=\{h: j\in\mathcal{F}_h\}$ that observe feature $j$:
\begin{equation}
\theta_j\leftarrow\sum_{h\in H_j}\frac{n_h}{\sum_{h'\in H_j}n_{h'}}\,\theta^{(h)}_j.
\end{equation}
This prevents embeddings of a feature from being diluted by nodes that never see it.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}
Calibration is summarized by the expected calibration error over $B$ equal-width probability bins,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECE}=\sum_{b=1}^{B}\frac{|S_b|}{N}\,\bigl|\mathrm{acc}(S_b)-\mathrm{conf}(S_b)\bigr|,
\end{equation}
and by the Brier score $\frac{1}{N}\sum_i(\hat y_i-y_i)^2$. Clinical utility at decision threshold $p_t$ is the net benefit
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NB}(p_t)=\frac{TP}{N}-\frac{FP}{N}\cdot\frac{p_t}{1-p_t},
\end{equation}
compared with the treat-all strategy $\mathrm{NB}_{\mathrm{all}}=\pi-(1-\pi)\,p_t/(1-p_t)$, where $\pi$ is prevalence~\cite{vickers2006}.

\section{Experimental Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
\textbf{Data partitioning and splits.} Each experimental track adheres to a prespecified partitioning protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Pooled multi-model benchmark ($N=74{,}013$):} Stratified five-fold cross-validation across all non-overlapping records (excluding Statlog). Within each fold, 80\% ($N=59{,}210$) serves as training data and 20\% ($N=14{,}803$) as the test set. Models are evaluated with raw features alone and with four engineered meta-features (\texttt{bmi}, \texttt{pulse\_pressure}, \texttt{chol\_tier}, \texttt{gluc\_tier}).
\item \emph{Literature 70/15/15 reproduction:} Stratified partition into 70\% training, 15\% validation (for early stopping checkpoint selection), and 15\% held-out test evaluation on the leaked pool (71{,}025 raw $\rightarrow$ 69{,}692 clean), deduplicated pool (70{,}302 raw $\rightarrow$ 68{,}969 clean), and Sulianova alone (68{,}568 full QC / 68{,}667 minimal filter).
\item \emph{Leave-one-cohort-out (LOCO) cross-hospital transfer:} In each of five folds, one entire clinical cohort is held out as the unseen target test cohort ($N_{\mathrm{test}}$: Hungarian=293, VA+Switzerland=308, Cleveland=303, Z-Alizadeh Sani=303, Framingham=4{,}238). The remaining $K-1$ cohorts form the source pool ($N_{\mathrm{source}}\approx 69{,}775$--$73{,}720$), strictly partitioned into a 70\% source training split ($D_{\mathrm{train}}$ for optimizing network parameters $\theta$), a 15\% source validation split ($D_{\mathrm{val}}$ for early stopping and temperature scaling $T^*$), and a disjoint 15\% calibration split ($D_{\mathrm{cal}}$ for finite-sample non-conformity threshold calibration $\hat{q}$). Zero-shot evaluation is executed on 100\% of target records. For target recalibration, a 20\% stratified local slice ($D_{\mathrm{tgt,cal}}$) re-estimates non-conformity quantiles, evaluated on the remaining 80\% target records.
\item \emph{Missingness stress test and architectural ablation:} Stratified 70\% training, 15\% validation, and 15\% held-out test multi-cohort partition ($N=11{,}102$ test patients, Statlog excluded).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Conformal calibration and decision rule.} For temperature-scaled probability $p_y(x) = \hat{P}(Y=y \mid X=x)$, non-conformity scores are $s(x,y)=1-p_y(x)$. At nominal coverage $1-\alpha=0.90$ ($\alpha=0.10$), the calibration quantile $\hat{q}$ is computed on $n$ calibration samples as $\hat{q} = \mathrm{Quantile}(\{s(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^n; \; \lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha) \rceil / n)$. The prediction set is $\mathcal{C}(x) = \{y \in \{0,1\} : s(x,y) \le \hat{q}\}$. A case is accepted when $|\mathcal{C}(x)|=1$ (decisive singleton with prediction $\hat{y}=\arg\max_y p_y(x)$), and referred/abstained when $|\mathcal{C}(x)|\in\{0,2\}$. Empirical set coverage satisfies Eq.~(\ref{eq:conformal_bound}). Mondrian conformal prediction computes $\hat{q}_g$ independently across biological sex $g\in\{\mathrm{Male},\mathrm{Female}\}$.

\textbf{Repeated seeds and variance reporting.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Missingness stress test (Table~\ref{tab:miss}):} To eliminate stochastic artifact from random masking, evaluations are repeated across \textbf{five independent random seeds} ($[42, 100, 2026, 777, 999]$). The same masked subsets are applied identically across all five model families (SchemaFlex, Unregularized Transformer, Native-NaN LightGBM, Native-NaN XGBoost, Median-Imputed LightGBM), reporting empirical mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation.
\item \emph{LOCO cross-cohort matrix (Table~\ref{tab:loco}) and conformal abstention audit (Table~\ref{tab:conf}):} Evaluated across \textbf{five independent random seeds} ($[42, 100, 2026, 777, 999]$) reporting empirical mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation across all cohorts, alongside deterministic single-seed ($42$) patient-level count audits and paired DeLong tests with family-wise Holm--Bonferroni correction.
\item \emph{Subgroup analysis:} Evaluated with 1{,}000 bootstrap resamples for 95\% confidence intervals.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Model architecture and training optimization.} \sfn{} employs 2 transformer layers, 4 attention heads, token embedding dimension $d=64$, feedforward dimension 128, and dropout $0.10$. Training applies AdamW ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), initial learning rate $1.5\times 10^{-3}$, cosine annealing decay down to $10^{-5}$ over 16 epochs, batch size 256, and gradient norm clipping at $1.0$. Loss regularization weights are fixed at $\lambda_{\mathrm{concept}}=0.10$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{mono}}=1.20$. Baseline tree models (LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost) are tuned with early stopping on validation AUROC. Prospective multi-site clinical validation remains recommended.

\subsection{Ablation and monotonicity}\label{sec:ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:abl} isolates each component on the held-out 15\% multi-cohort partition. We highlight an essential methodological distinction: Variant 1 (inner join, AUROC 0.6342) restricts input features to the 5 shared features, whereas Variant 2 (+ schema tokenizer, AUROC 0.8560) expands access to all 14 union features. To isolate feature access from model architecture, we evaluated a NaN-aware LightGBM on genuinely identical train, validation, and test partitions using equivalent feature preprocessing, which achieved AUROC 0.8510 (vs.\ 0.6280 on the inner join). Thus, schema retention (preserving specialized diagnostic variables) accounts for the vast majority ($\approx$+0.223 AUROC) of the observed gain, while the schema-flexible tokenizer provides an incremental $+0.0050$ AUROC alongside the structural capability to ingest dynamic subsets without static imputation. Given the modest incremental margin (+0.0050), this does not establish general architectural superiority of tabular transformers over gradient-boosted trees.
Feature dropout adds $+0.004$ AUROC and lowers monotonicity violations from 19.2\% to 4.2\%. The concept head changes AUROC by $+0.0007$ and raises violations to 13.0\%, confirming its value is interpretive rather than predictive. Finally, in Variant 5, the positive-constrained skip path and autograd gradient penalty ($\lambda_2=1.20$) independently regularize input directional gradients, suppressing counterfactual monotonicity violation rates to 0.26\% on synthetic $+0.5$~SD perturbations of SBP and cholesterol without compromising discriminative capacity (0.8652 AUROC). In contrast, post-hoc temperature scaling operates strictly on output logits to minimize probability calibration error (ECE 0.0572) and has zero effect on AUROC. These two mechanisms address orthogonal clinical objectives (gradient-level physiological plausibility vs.\ calibrated confidence) and should not be conflated as a single architectural intervention. These are single-run figures without confidence intervals, and the evaluation split differs from that of Table~\ref{tab:loco}.

\subsection{Conformal abstention and cohort shift}\label{sec:conformal}
At $\alpha=0.10$ the model accepts 46.5--77.7\% of patients across 5 seeds depending on the cohort (Table~\ref{tab:conf}). On accepted cases AUROC rises by 0.03--0.09 and accuracy by 5--12 points on four tertiary cardiology cohorts; on Framingham accuracy remains flat (51.3\% to 53.7\%) due to outcome construct divergence. The accepted fraction is a \emph{selective-prediction rate}, not conformal coverage.

Table~\ref{tab:conf} provides the multi-seed LOCO conformal evaluation (mean $\pm$ std across 5 repeated seeds) and the deterministic seed-42 patient-level count audit reconciling accepted accuracy and empirical set coverage under Eq.~(\ref{eq:conformal_bound}). Crucially, in all 25 experimental runs (5 seeds $\times$ 5 cohorts), no empty prediction sets occur ($N_0=0$); thus, the empirical set coverage matches the theoretical upper bound $1 - r_{\mathrm{acc}}(1 - \mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{acc}})$ identically. For instance, on the seed-42 audit for Hungarian ($N=293$), 213 singletons ($N_{1,\mathrm{corr}}=190$, $N_{1,\mathrm{err}}=23$, 89.2\% accuracy) and 80 pairs ($N_2=80, N_0=0$) yield $N_{\mathrm{cov}}=270$, giving empirical coverage $270/293 = 92.15\%$, identically matching $1 - (213/293)\times(1-0.8920) = 92.15\%$. On Cleveland ($N=303$), 212 singletons (177 correct, 35 errors) and 91 pairs yield $N_{\mathrm{cov}}=268$, giving empirical coverage $268/303 = 88.45\%$, matching the theoretical ceiling $88.45\%$. On Framingham ($N=4{,}238$), accepted accuracy is 49.19\% across 2{,}968 singletons (1,460 correct, 1,508 errors); with 1{,}270 pairs and $N_0=0$, empirical coverage is $2{,}730/4{,}238=64.42\%$, identically bounded by $64.42\%$. Across 5 seeds, source coverage averages 70.7\% on Framingham and 87.7--88.7\% on tertiary cohorts (unweighted mean $84.6\% \pm 2.2\%$), demonstrating that cross-cohort shift degrades marginal coverage below nominal 90\%. Target recalibration on a 20\% local slice restores coverage to $89.7$--$95.1\%$ ($91.9\% \pm 2.6\%$ overall) by adapting non-conformity quantiles to the target feature distribution.

\subsection{Robustness to missing data}
Table~\ref{tab:miss} and Fig.~\ref{fig:miss} show AUROC across 0--50\% test-time feature missingness on the held-out 15\% multi-cohort partition, evaluated on identical feature dropout masks across all models and averaged over 5 repeated random seeds ($\pm$ standard deviation). When trained on the complete 14-feature union schema, tree baselines at 0\% missingness achieve $\approx$0.811 AUROC (Native LightGBM 0.8107, Native XGBoost 0.8110, Median LightGBM 0.8105), fully consistent with the pooled cross-validation band of Table~\ref{tab:pooled}. Under test-time feature dropout, \sfn{} degrades from 0.8520 to 0.7123 $\pm$ 0.0051 at 50\% missingness, maintaining an advantage over split-routing native-NaN trees (+0.0279 over LightGBM, +0.0409 over XGBoost). However, simple median-imputed LightGBM retains 0.7262 $\pm$ 0.0019 AUROC, outperforming both native-NaN trees and the unregularized transformer. This empirical finding provides a crucial scientific qualification: token dropout equips transformers with structural adaptability to unobserved inputs, but simple mean or median imputation in tree ensembles remains remarkably robust under random missingness. Comparative robustness claims must therefore be labeled strictly provisional and task-dependent rather than indicative of general neural superiority.

\subsection{Fairness, federation and clinical utility}
\textbf{Subgroups.} On the 270-patient overlap cohort, male and female false-positive rates are 0.398 and 0.119 and sensitivities 0.86 and 0.80; the age $>65$ group has only 30 patients. In a separate 298-patient evaluation, marginal conformal calibration gave FPRs of 0.038 (male) and 0.186 (female); Mondrian calibration moved them to 0.173 and 0.246. It therefore \emph{halved} the inter-sex FPR gap, from 0.149 to 0.072 (a 51\% reduction), and lowered male FNR from 0.395 to 0.250, but it did not eliminate the gap and it raised the FPR of both groups. Subgroup samples are too small for confidence intervals to support firm conclusions.

\textbf{Federated simulation.} In a four-node simulation, schema-aware averaging (feature-specific parameters averaged only across nodes that observe the feature) reaches 0.767 AUROC after 10 rounds, versus 0.746 for isolated models and 0.875 for centralized training. Federation closes 16.3\% of the gap between isolated and centralized training. It shares only model weights, and no formal privacy mechanism (differential privacy, secure aggregation) was applied, so ``no row sharing'' should not be read as a privacy guarantee.

\textbf{Decision curves.} On the same 270-patient cohort (prevalence 44.4\%), net benefit exceeds treat-all at thresholds from 0.10 to 0.30, e.g.\ 0.346 vs.\ 0.306 at 0.20, equivalent to about four additional net true-positive decisions per 100 patients. This holds for one high-prevalence single-center cohort and should not be generalized.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{What the evidence supports.} Duplicate contamination and cross-cohort record overlap are real and widespread in popular files, invalidating random-split benchmarks. Inner-join harmonization severely penalizes cross-cohort transfer by discarding non-overlapping clinical measurements. A union-schema representation recovers most of this loss, whether through masked transformers or NaN-aware trees; the transformer adds a modest mean gain ($+0.023$ AUROC) that is not statistically significant per cohort after Holm correction, provides structural robustness against test-time missingness, and enables conformal abstention, but does not achieve uniform superiority.

\textbf{What it does not support.} We do not show that duplicate contamination explains the published 90.1\% in~\cite{mahmud2026}; both fixed-prediction bounds and controlled retraining indicate that duplicates alter accuracy by only $\sim$0.30 percentage points, leaving the origin of that figure undetermined. We do not claim an architecture-faithful refutation of the proprietary transformer in~\cite{mahmud2026}, nor do we claim statistical equivalence to union-schema LightGBM.

\subsection{Outcome-construct divergence}
A central validity limitation in multi-cohort cardiovascular benchmarks is outcome construct divergence. The merged binary label conflates three clinically and temporally distinct targets:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Prevalent cardiovascular disease at examination} (Sulianova CVD, $N{=}68{,}568$): cross-sectional clinical history and screening findings.
\item \emph{Angiographic coronary artery disease} (UCI cohorts, Z-Alizadeh Sani, $N{=}1{,}207$): acute objective visualization of $\ge 50\%$ luminal stenosis on cardiac catheterization.
\item \emph{Incident coronary heart disease events during longitudinal follow-up} (Framingham Study, $N{=}4{,}238$): 10-year prospective event occurrence~\cite{dawber1951}.
\end{enumerate}
These targets share a binary encoding but fundamentally represent different clinical prediction problems: acute anatomical stenosis detection versus 10-year prospective survival prognosis. This construct divergence directly explains the flat transfer performance on Framingham (AUROC $\approx 0.670$--$0.680$ across all models) and the collapse in conformal set coverage to 70.7\% $\pm$ 5.3\% (seed-42: 64.4\%), where singleton accuracy is only 53.7\% $\pm$ 5.9\% (seed-42: 49.2\%). Multi-cohort benchmarks should avoid pooling distinct clinical targets or employ construct-specific prediction heads.

\textbf{Limitations.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Outcome construct heterogeneity}, as analyzed above; conflating cross-sectional examination findings with 10-year incident events restricts clinical validity of pooled predictions.
\item \emph{Pool composition imbalance.} Sulianova contributes 92.6\% of records, meaning learning of specialized cardiology variables depends on a small minority of patients.
\item \emph{Conformal validity under cohort shift.} Source calibration yields coverage of 70.7\% on Framingham and 87.7--88.7\% on tertiary cohorts (84.6\% $\pm$ 2.2\% unweighted mean; seed-42: 64.4--92.2\%) against nominal 90\%, rigorously bounded by accepted accuracy under Eq.~(\ref{eq:conformal_bound}); conformal guarantees fail under distribution shift without local adaptation.
\item \emph{Statistical power and equivalence testing.} Modest sample sizes ($N\approx 300$) limit power to detect small AUROC differences; lack of significance after Holm correction does not demonstrate practical equivalence.
\item \emph{Comparative tree robustness is provisional and context-dependent.} While \sfn{} exceeds native-NaN split-routing trees at 50\% missingness (0.712 vs.\ 0.684), median-imputed LightGBM achieves 0.726, demonstrating that tree models with simple imputation remain competitive under missing data and that neural models do not possess general missingness superiority.
\item \emph{Ablation interpretation.} Schema retention accounts for the vast majority of the gain ($\approx$+0.223 AUROC), with the tokenizer architecture contributing an incremental $+0.0050$ AUROC. Furthermore, monotonicity regularization and post-hoc temperature scaling address orthogonal clinical objectives (gradient plausibility vs.\ probability calibration) and should not be conflated.
\item \emph{Reproduction scope.} Our 70/15/15 reproduction demonstrates that standard tabular models achieve $\approx$73\% on the published features, but does not constitute an architecture-faithful reproduction of unreleased neural code.
\item \emph{Patient identity verification.} Feature signature matching demonstrates record-level duplicate overlap but cannot conclusively prove biological identity without hospital records.
\item \emph{No prospective clinical validation.} The model is an investigative benchmark prototype, not an approved diagnostic instrument.
\end{enumerate}

%=====================================================================
\section{Recommended Practice for Cardiovascular Benchmarks}\label{sec:practice}
Our audit suggests a short checklist that would have prevented the problems documented above and costs little to adopt.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Deduplicate before splitting}, using exact and near-duplicate signature matching on clinical features, and report the number removed per source.
\item \emph{Split at the patient level} and, where several files derive from one registry, check for overlap across files before any cross-cohort evaluation.
\item \emph{Report the pooled composition}: the share of records contributed by each source, and the outcome definition used for each.
\item \emph{Benchmark against the strongest tree baseline} with native missing-value handling and with the same engineered features as the proposed model.
\item \emph{Evaluate on a held-out cohort}, not only on a random split of the pool, and give intervals and seed variance.
\item \emph{Report conformal set coverage and acceptance rate separately}, and check coverage on a different cohort before claiming safety.
\item \emph{Reconcile every reported number}: text, tables and figures (e.g.\ confusion matrices) should imply identical metrics.
\item \emph{Publish code, splits and an audit log}, following TRIPOD+AI~\cite{collins2024}.
\end{enumerate}
We emphasize that items 1--2 are enough, by themselves, to remove the most extreme inflation seen in public heart-disease files (Table~\ref{tab:leak}).

%=====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Headline accuracies on public cardiovascular benchmarks should be read with suspicion until duplicates, cross-cohort overlap and schema loss are audited. After cleaning, realistic accuracy on pooled routine data is about 73--76\%, and an empirical 70/15/15 reproduction of a published 90.1\% setup using standard tabular baselines gives about 73\% whether or not the duplicates are kept (though this does not refute unreleased proprietary neural code). Cross-hospital transfer depends mostly on preserving unshared variables rather than on architectural novelty. \sfn{} offers a principled way to train across non-identical schemas, with a modest mean gain over a strong NaN-aware tree baseline that is not significant per cohort after Holm correction. At 50\% test-time feature missingness, it exceeds native-NaN LightGBM and XGBoost by approximately 0.028 and 0.041 AUROC, respectively, but is outperformed by median-imputed LightGBM (0.726), confirming that general superiority under missingness is not established. Furthermore, its conformal coverage guarantee weakens under cohort shift. Its claims must be tightened by multi-seed evaluation and prospective validation. We release the audit log, schema registry and code to support independent verification.
